# *Book 5 - Make Copilot Part of Your Work*

> *Book 5* is online-only because you do not need to read all of it, you only need to read the role chapter closest to your own work. I did not want my readers to have to pay for material they won't use.

You've now used every tool *Microsoft Copilot Visual Bible* covers. You can ask a focused question, check a source, draft a document, analyze a table, recap a meeting, and build an automation with an approval step. What remains is to make these part of how you work: a small number of dependable routines that fit your own job, that you keep using after the novelty has gone, and that you can show are worth the time.

![Book 5 hero figure](Book%205%20hero.png)

*Book 5* does that in three parts. First, you'll take stock of your own repeated work and choose where Copilot fits. Next, you'll see worked examples for different roles, so you can borrow the ones closest to yours. Finally, you'll learn how to protect the people affected by your use of Copilot, how to measure whether it's helping, and how to adopt one new routine in 30 days.

# What you'll learn in *Book 5*

*Book 5* has twelve chapters:

- **Chapter 5.1** helps you build a personal Copilot work system: an inventory of your repeated work, and a short list of where to start.
- **Chapters 5.2 to 5.9** each cover a role or a group of roles: managers and project leaders; administrators and executive assistants; analysts, finance, and operations; marketing and communication; sales and customer success; HR and recruitment; educators and trainers; and writers, researchers, consultants, and freelancers.
- **Chapter 5.10** sets out how to protect people, information, and professional trust in any role.
- **Chapter 5.11** shows you how to measure Copilot's value, including what it costs.
- **Chapter 5.12** is a 30-day plan that takes one recurring problem from first idea to a measured, working routine.

# How to read *Book 5*

You don't need to read every chapter. Read *Chapter 5.1* first. Then read the role chapter closest to your own work, and one or two others that touch it. A team leader in a marketing department might read *Chapters 5.2* and *5.5*. A freelance trainer might read *Chapters 5.8* and *5.9*. Finish with *Chapters 5.10* to *5.12*, which apply to everyone.

Each role chapter follows the same pattern:

- **Where Copilot fits** in the role, and where it doesn't.
- **Workflows**, each shown as a short card with the trigger, the inputs, the tool, the check, and what stays with you.
- **The risks particular to the role**, and how to handle them.
- **A Try it now activity** that sets up one of the workflows with your own work.

The role chapters don't teach features again. They point back to the chapters where you learned each one. If a technique is unfamiliar, follow the reference.

# The example changes

*Book 1* to *Book 4* followed one example, the four-day work week, so that you could see each skill build on the last. *Book 5* leaves it behind. From here on, the example is your own work.

The role chapters use short, fictional scenarios to show each workflow. Treat them as patterns. Your files, your colleagues, and your organization's rules are different, and the workflows will need adjusting to fit.

> **Sample files**: The book's GitHub repository has a `Book5` folder with three templates: `work-inventory.xlsx` for *Chapter 5.1*, `value-tracker.xlsx` for *Chapter 5.11*, and `adoption-plan.docx` for *Chapter 5.12*: https://github.com/markjprice/copilot-vb/tree/main/files/Book5

# Which plan *Book 5* assumes

*Book 5* introduces no new features, so the plan notes from earlier chapters still apply. In brief:

- Workflows that use Chat, files, web search, Pages, and Notebooks work with every plan covered in this book.
- Workflows that use Researcher, Analyst, or Cowork need Microsoft 365 Premium or Pro, or a Microsoft 365 Copilot license at work.
- Workflows that use Teams meetings, shared mailboxes and calendars, or SharePoint need a work account.

Each workflow card says which tool it uses. Where a tool needs a particular plan, the card gives a simpler alternative.

One feature gets a first mention here. Microsoft is testing a **Today** view in Copilot Home that brings together email, meetings, and tasks you may have missed. It's in private preview, so most readers won't have it yet. *Chapter 5.1* shows you how to get the same result with a scheduled prompt.

# What you need before you start

Before you begin, make sure you have:

- The skills from *Book 1* to *Book 4* because *Book 5* refers to them constantly.
- A view of your own calendar and task list for the last few weeks, so you can see what you repeat.
- About 20 to 30 minutes for each chapter you read, and a little time each working day for the 30-day plan in *Chapter 5.12*.
